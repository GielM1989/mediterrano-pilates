## 1. Terminology: which French words do studio owners and buyers use?

### Takeaway
French retail and B2B pages for the sock use "chaussettes antidérapantes" (dominant) and "chaussettes (de) Pilates". "Grip" shows up only as an English loan in brand or model names, and "à picots" appears only in product descriptions ("picots en silicone"). Reformer is "Pilates Reformer" or "Pilates sur machine (Reformer)". The ring is sold as both "anneau de Pilates" and "cercle de Pilates". Software vendors call the studio's customers "membres", "clients" or "adhérents". The best combined head term for the grip-sock page is "chaussettes antidérapantes personnalisées" plus "Pilates" in the H1.

### Inferences
Recommended term set (inference from the above; no volumes measured):
- **Grip socks:** primary "chaussettes antidérapantes" (always pair with "Pilates" in the H1). Secondary "chaussettes de Pilates" / "chaussettes Pilates". Tertiary "chaussettes grip" once in body/FAQ, because younger studios and the English-name retailers use it. Use "picots en silicone" / "semelle à picots en silicone" in specs. Avoid "chaussettes à picots" as a heading: it reads as a description, not a category name.
- **Custom:** "personnalisées" (main), "à votre logo" / "avec votre logo" (benefit phrasing), "sur mesure" (secondary). Technique: "logo tissé (jacquard)" and "logo brodé", explicitly "jamais imprimé". In French socks are knitted, so "tricoté" is technically accurate. Write "logo tissé en jacquard dans la maille" once, and keep "tissé" so it matches the "woven" claim in the brand facts. Do not write "imprimé".
- **Merch:** use "merchandising" (B2B, mirrors ES/DE) and "produits à votre logo / à l'image de votre studio" for revenue framing. "Produits dérivés" fits the resale angle and suits the "revenue, not cost" argument. Use "goodies", "objets publicitaires" and "cadeaux d'entreprise" at most once (FAQ or blog), because they signal free giveaways: the opposite of the brand's argument.
- **Tumbler 1,2 L:** H1/heading "gourde isotherme 1,2 L personnalisée", with "tumbler" in parentheses or in the body, since the French market term for Stanley-style cups is likely the loanword "tumbler" (not verified with a fetched source). Avoid "gobelet" (reads as a disposable cup) and "thermos" (brand-derived).
- **Tote bag:** "tote bag personnalisé" (masculine in French: "un tote bag"), with "sac en toile" / "sac en coton" as a synonym in the body. This is an inference: no French tote-bag source was fetched.
- **Ring:** H1 "anneau de Pilates personnalisé", with "cercle de Pilates (magic circle)" in the first paragraph. Decathlon uses both terms, but "anneau" appears more often in product titles.
- **Studio:** "studio de Pilates", "studio de Pilates Reformer", "studio de yoga", "studio de barre". Capitalise "Pilates" in copy, as FPMP does and as a proper noun.
- **Reformer:** "Pilates Reformer" (main), "Pilates sur machine" (synonym; use once).
- **Studio's customers:** the ES site's "alumnas" maps best to "élèves" (gender-neutral, used by teachers) for loyalty, welcome-pack and Christmas contexts. Use "clients" (bsport/Appvizer wording) for revenue and resale contexts, and "adhérents" for subscription or membership contexts. Never use "étudiantes" (MT error for "alumnas") or "pratiquants" as a main term (sounds federation or sport-club).
- **Studio owner:** "gérant·e / fondatrice de studio", or simply "vous". Avoid "propriétaire de studio", which literally reads as the landlord.
- **Mockup:** "maquette" (with "mockup" once in parentheses). French B2B print uses "BAT (bon à tirer)" for the approval proof; this could appear in the how-it-works steps, but it is unverified usage here.
- **Free shipping:** "livraison offerte" (common French e-commerce wording) or "livraison gratuite en Europe".

## 3. Register and tone: vous or tu?

### Takeaway
Use **vouvoiement** throughout. Every French studio-software and B2B source checked addresses the studio owner with "vous". The FR site should still sound warm and direct (short sentences, "votre studio", "vos élèves"), not stiff.

### Cited Findings
- bsport (French studio software) is described with the second-person plural: "garantir que votre studio fonctionne sans accroc". Skello quotes it as "Parfait si vous proposez des cours en visio et en présentiel." — [Appvizer](https://www.appvizer.fr/loisirs/studio-yoga/bsport); [Skello](https://www.skello.io/blog/logiciels-gestion-salle-sport)
- Resamania's pricing copy uses the imperative vouvoiement: "Ajoutez 70€ pour le contrôle d'accès". — [Skello](https://www.skello.io/blog/logiciels-gestion-salle-sport)
- bsport's own .io FR page could not be fetched (blocked); its English site headline is "YOUR BOUTIQUE STUDIO, OUR INDUSTRY-LEADING TECH". — [bsport](https://bsport.io)

### Inferences
- Vouvoiement is the safe default for a foreign supplier writing to business owners it has not met. Tutoiement is used by some lifestyle/DTC brands towards consumers, but no B2B studio-software or supplier source in this run used it. A Spanish company using "tu" risks reading as a machine translation of the Spanish "tú". The ES site's informal register should not be carried over.
- Tone markers for FR: "Votre logo, tissé dans la chaussette." / "Vous revendez, vous gardez la marge." Keep the brand rules: no exclamation marks, no superlatives ("le meilleur", "incroyable"), no discount language ("promo", "offre exceptionnelle"). French B2B also tolerates fewer claims than English; one factual sentence beats two adjectives.
- Gender-inclusive writing: the point médian ("élève·s", "gérant·e·s") is politically marked in France and banned in some official contexts. Prefer epicene words ("élèves", "équipe", "studio") and avoid the point médian on commercial pages (inference; not sourced in this run).

- Deciplus' and Resamania's own sites were not fetched directly; findings come via review sites (Skello, Appvizer).
- No French Pilates equipment supplier site (e.g. the Balanced Body or Align-Pilates distributors Sissel France or Stelvoren) was checked for vous/tu.

## 4. French typography rules and machine-translation pitfalls

### Takeaway
French (France) requires a non-breaking space before : ; ? ! and %, inside « », and before € ("6,95 €"). It also needs a decimal comma, a space as the thousands separator, accents on capitals ("À propos", "É"), and sentence-case titles. B2B prices should be labelled HT, with the resale price in TTC.

### Cited Findings
- Deux-points, point-virgule, point d'exclamation, point d'interrogation: "une espace insécable avant, un espace après". — [Blog du Modérateur, règles de typographie](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/regles-ponctuation-typographie/)
- Guillemets « »: at the opening, an ordinary space before and a non-breaking space after; at the closing, a non-breaking space before. — [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/regles-ponctuation-typographie/)
- Percentage and mathematical signs: non-breaking space before ("100 %"). — [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/regles-ponctuation-typographie/)
- Thousands separator: a space every three digits ("1 000 000"), but none in years ("1950") or phone numbers. Decimals take a comma ("3,14"), never a point. — [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/regles-ponctuation-typographie/)
- Capitals keep their accents (Académie française: "l'accent a pleine valeur orthographique"). Months, days and languages are lowercase; holidays are capitalised ("Noël"). — [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/regles-ponctuation-typographie/)
- French retail price format: "14,99 €" (comma decimal, € after the number with a space). — [Decathlon.fr search snippet, Anneau Pilates Ring](https://www.decathlon.fr/p/mp/pack-anneau-de-fitness-demontable-et-recouvert-de-mousse-et-gaine-de-sport-aminc/2393d471-ecf4-4a42-9dca-a38d24ed9694/c255)
- Typography references exist from France (Imprimerie nationale tradition, via Debian's French typography page) and from Québec (OQLF). ### Inferences
Concrete build rules (inference / standard practice; implement in templates):
- In HTML use U+00A0 (`&nbsp;`) or the narrow no-break space U+202F (`&#8239;`) before : ; ! ? % € and inside « ». Many French publishers use the narrow space before ; ! ?. Pick one and apply it consistently, ideally via a build-time regex, because MT and CMS output usually inserts normal spaces, which causes line breaks before "?".
- Prices: "6,95 € HT la paire", "prix de revente conseillé : 19,95 € TTC", "minimum 50 paires", "1,2 L", "24 h", "4 semaines". With 20 % VAT in France, 19,95 € TTC equals 16,63 € HT (rounded). Only show this if the brand facts allow derived numbers. BE (21 %), LU (17 %) and CH (8,1 %, per project doc europees-groeiplan-v2) have different VAT, so say "TTC (TVA française)" or keep the resale price as "conseillé".
- Write "HT" and "TTC" in capitals without periods. On a B2B site always mark the primary price "HT" (French B2B price display is conventionally excl. VAT).
- Titles and H1/H2 in sentence case ("Comment choisir un fournisseur", not "Comment Choisir Un Fournisseur"). English title case is a giveaway of MT.
- Write "e-mail" (France; "mail" is colloquial). "Courriel" is the Québec/official form and sounds odd on a French commercial site. Belgium: "GSM" for mobile; use "téléphone" or "WhatsApp" to stay neutral.
- Numbers: digits for all product facts; never "septante/nonante" (BE/CH) or "soixante-dix". Use "1er", "2e" (not "2ème").
- Use "Pantone" (brand name, capital P), "conforme REACH" / "testées REACH", "semelle en silicone" (le silicone).
- Times: "24 h" (with space) or "sous 24 heures"; dates "15 octobre 2026".

Common MT errors to block (inference from typical DeepL/Google output for ES→FR / EN→FR):
- "grip socks" → "chaussettes de préhension" / "chaussettes à poignée"; "tumbler" → "gobelet" / "verre"; "tote bag" → "sac fourre-tout" (acceptable but not the term buyers use); "mockup" → "maquette" is fine, but avoid "simulation" / "prototype".
- "alumnas" → "étudiantes"/"élèves femmes" (use "élèves"); "estudio" → "atelier"; "dueña del estudio" → "propriétaire" (use "gérante/fondatrice" or "vous").
- "margen" → fine as "marge"; "ingresos" → "revenus" (prefer "chiffre d'affaires" or "recettes" in business context).
- "free shipping" → "expédition libre" (wrong); use "livraison offerte".
- "woven" → "tressé" (wrong); use "tissé (jacquard)".
- Leftover Spanish punctuation (¿ ¡), English quotes "…" instead of « … », decimal points (6.95 €), € before the number (€6,95), missing accents on capitals (A propos → À propos).
- Tu/vous mixing within one page (DeepL tends to switch); fix with DeepL formality = "more" or a glossary.

## 6. Google AI Overviews / AI Mode in France and AI-assistant usage

### Takeaway
AI Overviews and AI Mode went live in France on **22 July 2026**, the last major Western European market to get them. No French data on which page types get cited was found. Generative AI is used by 48 % of French people; among users, ChatGPT dominates (79 %) ahead of Gemini (31 %) and Mistral's Le Chat (14 %), and information search is the top use.

### Cited Findings
- Google began rolling out AI Overviews and AI Mode in France on Wednesday 22 July 2026, on the web and in the Google app (iOS/Android), after a two-year delay. AI Mode is a separate tab next to Images/Actualités/Vidéos. — [Blog du Modérateur](https://blogdumoderateur.com/google-deploie-ai-overviews-ai-mode-france); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-ends-france-ai-overviews-blackout-as-68-users-flagged-the-gap)

- Publisher context: neighbouring-rights framework, €250 M fine by the Autorité de la concurrence (March 2024). Google's compromise lets publishers opt out of AI Overviews without losing ranking and pays about 450 contracted media outlets when their content appears. A page-level exclusion control for informational pages is due 3 March 2027. — [Blog du Modérateur](https://blogdumoderateur.com/google-deploie-ai-overviews-ai-mode-france); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-ends-france-ai-overviews-blackout-as-68-users-flagged-the-gap)
