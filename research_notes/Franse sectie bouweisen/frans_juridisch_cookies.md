## 1. Mentions légales: what French law requires, what applies to a company established in Spain, and how to handle the missing street address

### Takeaway
Since the SREN law (23 May 2024) the French publisher-identification duty sits in LCEN art. 1-1 (no longer art. 6 III); e-commerce information is in LCEN art. 19. But for a company established in Spain, the e-Commerce Directive's country-of-origin principle means Spanish law (LSSI art. 10) governs the information duty, and both Spanish law and the EU directive require a geographic address. Leaving the street address out is therefore a gap under Spanish law as well, not only French; the honest fix is to publish the domicilio social currently on file at the Registro Mercantil (still legally valid until the change is registered) and update it later.

### Cited Findings
- The SREN law (loi n° 2024-449 of 21 May 2024) moved the mandatory publisher identification mentions from LCEN art. 6 III to **LCEN art. 1-1**; art. 6 now covers intermediary services; sanctions are in **art. 1-2**; in force since 23 May 2024 — [Simonnet Avocat](https://www.simonnetavocat.fr/mentions-legales-dun-site-internet-obligations-sanctions-et-modele/); SREN law text: [Légifrance JORF](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/article_jo/JORFARTI000049563632)
- Art. 1-1 items for a legal person: dénomination or raison sociale, adresse du siège social, numéro de téléphone; RCS/RNE registration number and share capital if registered; directeur de la publication (must be the legal representative); hébergeur's name, address and phone; where applicable the subcontractors that store the publisher's data — [Simonnet Avocat](https://www.simonnetavocat.fr/mentions-legales-dun-site-internet-obligations-sanctions-et-modele/)
- E-mail address and intra-EU VAT number come from **LCEN art. 19** (e-commerce); VAT number only if liable to VAT — [Simonnet Avocat](https://www.simonnetavocat.fr/mentions-legales-dun-site-internet-obligations-sanctions-et-modele/)
- Sanctions (art. 1-2): natural person 1 year imprisonment and EUR 75,000; legal person EUR 375,000 plus complementary penalties — [Simonnet Avocat](https://www.simonnetavocat.fr/mentions-legales-dun-site-internet-obligations-sanctions-et-modele/); same amounts on the French Ministry of Economy page — [economie.gouv.fr (11/12/2025)](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/developper-son-entreprise/innover-et-numeriser-son-entreprise/mentions-sur-votre-site-internet-les-obligations-respecter)
- French Ministry of Economy checklist for a société: dénomination sociale, forme juridique, capital; siège social address, e-mail, phone; IP ownership of images; host's name, company name, address and phone "même si l'hébergement est gratuit"; for commercial activities: RCS/RNE number and intra-EU VAT number; e-commerce sites: CGV; information sites: directeur de la publication — [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/developper-son-entreprise/innover-et-numeriser-son-entreprise/mentions-sur-votre-site-internet-les-obligations-respecter)
- Refusing to communicate CGV to a professional client: fine EUR 15,000 (individual) / EUR 75,000 (company) — [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/developper-son-entreprise/innover-et-numeriser-son-entreprise/mentions-sur-votre-site-internet-les-obligations-respecter)
- Personal data information duty (identity/contact of controller, legal basis, purpose, recipients, retention, rights incl. complaint to CNIL, transfers outside EU) and cookie consent are listed as website obligations on the same page — [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/developper-son-entreprise/innover-et-numeriser-son-entreprise/mentions-sur-votre-site-internet-les-obligations-respecter)

### Inferences
- **Country of origin [unverified, but standard EU law]:** Directive 2000/31/EC art. 3 (coordinated field) makes the information society service subject to the law of the Member State of establishment (Spain). The general information duty (art. 5 Directive) is implemented in Spain by **LSSI (Ley 34/2002) art. 10**. So, strictly, LCEN art. 1-1/19 do not bind Ecomtierra; LSSI art. 10 does. Directive art. 5(1)(b) requires "the geographic address at which the service provider is established" and LSSI art. 10.1.a requires "domicilio"; art. 10.1.b requires Registro Mercantil inscription data. The current Spanish/EN/DE/NL legal pages (no address, no registry data) are therefore probably incomplete under Spanish law too. Switzerland is outside the directive (see Q7).
- **Honest handling of the address:** the address on file at the Registro Mercantil remains the legally valid siège social until the change is registered (and it is already public via the Registro Mercantil/BORME). Best option: publish that registered address now, labelled "Siège social (adresse enregistrée au Registro Mercantil)", and update on registration. If the owner refuses to show any address, a weaker fallback is: city/province + "Espagne" + "adresse complète du siège communiquée sur simple demande et figurant sur nos factures" — this is transparent but does not meet the "geographic address" requirement; flag as residual risk. Do not invent or show a virtual/placeholder address.
- **Phone:** LCEN art. 1-1 lists a phone number; LSSI requires e-mail "y cualquier otro dato que permita establecer con él una comunicación directa y efectiva" [unverified wording]. A phone or WhatsApp business number is expected by French B2B buyers and strengthens compliance in both systems.

## 2. Cookies: CNIL rules and whether GA4 with Consent Mode v2 "denied by default" is compliant

### Takeaway
CNIL requires refusal to be as easy as acceptance on the first layer, purposes and the list of controllers to be shown, withdrawal as easy as consent, proof of consent, and recommends keeping both acceptance and refusal for 6 months. GA4 in Consent Mode v2 *advanced* (gtag loaded, cookieless pings sent while denied) is very likely **not** covered by the CNIL audience-measurement exemption and the pings themselves fall within the scope of the consent rule per EDPB guidelines; the safe setup for French/Belgian/Luxembourg visitors is Consent Mode *basic* (no Google request at all before "Accepter").

### Cited Findings
- CNIL consolidated cookie recommendation published 16 Jan 2026, combining deliberation 2020-092 (17 Sept 2020) and deliberation 2025-131 (18 Dec 2025); 2025 changes add section 7 on optional multi-device consent in logged-in environments — [CNIL, recommandation consolidée (PDF)](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)
- Acceptance and refusal must be offered "avec le même degré de simplicité"; CNIL "recommande fortement" refusal on the same screen; example: "tout accepter" / "tout refuser" buttons at the same level and format; "continuer sans accepter" accepted as a refusal method; closing the window may mean refusal only if "clairement indiqué"; same size/prominence — [CNIL recommandation §2.3](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)
- Purposes must be shown before the choice, each with a short heading and brief description, detailed description easily accessible; full, regularly updated list of controllers available at the time of consent (may be on a second layer, number of controllers may be shown first) — [CNIL recommandation §2.1–2.2](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)
- Keeping the choice for 6 months is "une bonne pratique", applying to "tant le consentement que le refus" — [CNIL recommandation §2.3](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)
- Proof: controllers must be able to demonstrate consent "à tout moment" (e.g. timestamped records, CMP logs, hashed code version) — [CNIL recommandation §4](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)
- Withdrawal: "il doit être aussi simple de retirer son consentement que de le donner"; recommended permanent link or cookie icon (e.g. bottom-left) — [CNIL recommandation §3](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)
- When refused: "aucune opération de lecture ou d'écriture soumise au consentement ne peut légalement avoir lieu" — [CNIL recommandation §2](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)
- Audience-measurement exemption: purpose "strictement limitée à la seule mesure de l'audience" for the publisher's sole account; anonymous statistics only; no cross-referencing with other processing, no transmission of non-anonymous data to third parties, no cross-site tracking; recommended: inform users (e.g. privacy policy), tracker lifetime about 13 months without automatic renewal, data retention max 25 months; offers where the provider reuses data for its own purposes fall outside; watch for transfers outside the EU. Page dated 4 July 2025; it does not mention Google Analytics or Consent Mode — [CNIL, solutions pour la mesure d'audience](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)
- CNIL runs an evaluation programme for consent-exempt audience-measurement solutions — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/solutions-de-mesure-daudience-exemptees-de-consentement-la-cnil-lance-un-programme-devaluation)
- Google: in advanced mode tags load with defaults `denied` and send "measurements without cookies"; in basic mode "no data is transferred to Google at all" without consent; with analytics_storage denied tags "Won't read or write first-party analytics cookies"; pings can include timestamp, user agent, referrer, consent state, a random number per page load; used "for basic measurement and modeling purposes" — [Google, Consent mode overview](https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/concepts/consent-mode?hl=en)
- EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0 (adopted 7 Oct 2024): "gaining access" under ePrivacy Art. 5(3) includes JavaScript where the entity "instructs the browser of the user to send asynchronous requests" — "Such access clearly falls within the scope of Article 5(3) ePD" (para. 33); tracking pixels constitute storage and "gaining of access" (paras. 50–51) — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 (PDF)](https://edpb.europa.eu:443/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)
- A French privacy publication (25 July 2026, updated 27 Sept 2026) argues "Le label « mode avancé » ne crée d'exemption à aucun de ces textes" and "« Sans cookies » ne signifie pas « sans données » ni « anonyme »"; it cites no CNIL position specific to Consent Mode — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://donneespersonnelles.fr/consent-mode-v2-rgpd)

### Inferences
- **GA4 + Consent Mode v2 advanced likely fails the CNIL exemption:** (a) the cookieless pings are sent by gtag JavaScript from the user's browser, which the EDPB says falls under Art. 5(3) even without cookies; (b) Google uses the pings for "modeling", i.e. not strictly for the publisher's own anonymous statistics, and CNIL excludes offers where the provider reuses data; (c) GA4 is not on any CNIL list of exempted solutions found in this session. Conclusion: treat GA4 as consent-based. Also loading gtag.js itself from Google's servers before consent sends the IP address to Google.
- **Recommended setup (for FR/BE/LU; simplest for whole site):** switch to Consent Mode **basic**: do not load gtag.js until "Accepter" (keep `gtag('consent','default',{...denied})` + load only after accept, then `consent update` granted). Keep the Meta Pixel as is (already loads only after accept). This also matches the existing banner text ("we measure only after you accept") honestly.
- If the owner wants exempt measurement for refusers, use a cookieless EU-hosted tool evaluated by CNIL (e.g. Matomo configured per CNIL guide) instead of GA4 [unverified product details].
- **Banner requirements in practice:** two equally styled buttons "Tout accepter" / "Tout refuser" (or "Accepter" / "Refuser"), optional "Personnaliser"; show the two purposes (mesure d'audience; publicité/mesure des campagnes Meta) and name Google and Meta; link to the politique de confidentialité; store the choice 6 months (both values) then ask again; keep a timestamped consent log (proof); permanent "Gérer les cookies" link in the footer to reopen the banner. Because there are only two non-essential purposes, a global accept/refuse with a "Personnaliser" layer offering per-purpose toggles is the cleanest way to meet granularity [inference from CNIL §2.1].
- **Proof of consent on a static Netlify site:** the CNIL wants demonstrability. A minimal approach: store in localStorage the choice, timestamp and banner version; log consent events (e.g. via a Netlify function or GA "consent" event after acceptance). Logging refusals server-side would itself be processing — keep minimal (timestamp, version, choice, no IP) [inference].
- **Draft French banner text:**
  - Titre : « Cookies et mesure d'audience »
  - Texte : « Nous utilisons des cookies pour mesurer l'audience du site (Google Analytics) et mesurer l'efficacité de nos publicités (Meta Pixel). Ces outils ne sont activés qu'avec votre accord. Vous pouvez accepter, refuser ou personnaliser vos choix, et les modifier à tout moment via le lien « Gestion des cookies » en bas de page. Votre choix est conservé 6 mois. En savoir plus : Politique de confidentialité. »
  - Boutons (même taille, même style) : « Tout refuser » · « Tout accepter » · lien « Personnaliser »
  - Couche « Personnaliser » : « Mesure d'audience — Google Analytics 4 (Google Ireland Ltd / Google LLC) : statistiques de fréquentation. Durée : 13 mois maximum. » / « Publicité — Meta Pixel (Meta Platforms Ireland Ltd) : mesure et optimisation de nos campagnes sur Facebook et Instagram. » / « Cookies nécessaires — mémorisation de votre choix (toujours actifs). »
- GA4 retention: set GA4 data retention to 14 months (GA4 options are 2 or 14 months [unverified]), consistent with CNIL's max 25 months; cookie `_ga` default lifetime is 2 years [unverified] — can be set to 13 months via `cookie_expires: 34128000` in gtag config to align with CNIL's 13-month recommendation.

Because Ecomtierra's only establishment is in Spain, the AEPD is the lead authority, but users may complain to their own authority (CNIL, APD, CNPD).

## 4. B2B terms (CGV) for a Spanish seller to French (and BE/LU/CH) professionals

### Takeaway
French CGV-content rules (L441-1 communication duty, L441-10 penalties and EUR 40 indemnity) are rules addressed to sellers under French law; a Spanish seller who validly chooses Spanish law is primarily bound by Spanish Ley 3/2004, which implements the same EU Late Payment Directive (2011/7/EU) and gives the same tools: automatic default interest (ECB + 8 points) and a fixed EUR 40 recovery indemnity. So one clause citing both the EUR 40 and ECB+8 is safe in all four markets. Whether French payment-term rules are overriding mandatory provisions (lois de police) for foreign sellers was not settled by any source found.

### Cited Findings
- Spanish Ley 3/2004 applies to all payments in commercial transactions between companies (art. 3); default payment term 30 days after receipt of goods, extendable by agreement but never beyond 60 days (art. 4); default arises "automáticamente por el mero incumplimiento" without notice (art. 5); statutory default interest = ECB main refinancing rate "más ocho puntos porcentuales", published every semester in the BOE (art. 7); fixed EUR 40 recovery costs "que se añadirá en todo caso", plus proven higher costs (art. 8); clauses "manifiestamente abusiva en perjuicio del acreedor" are void (art. 9) — [BOE, Ley 3/2004 consolidada](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2004-21830)
- French Cour de cassation (com., 24 Apr 2024, n° 22-24.275): the late-payment penalty of L441-10 II "constitue un intérêt moratoire" and does not cumulate with statutory late interest; it did not address foreign law or lois de police; it relied on Directive 2011/7/EU — [Cour de cassation decision via CURIA (PDF)](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-04/france_cour_de_cassation_24.04.2024_n22-24.275.pdf)
- Refusing to communicate CGV to a professional buyer who asks for them is sanctioned in France (EUR 75,000 for a company) — [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/developper-son-entreprise/innover-et-numeriser-son-entreprise/mentions-sur-votre-site-internet-les-obligations-respecter)

### Inferences
- **Applicable law [unverified, standard]:** Rome I Regulation art. 3 allows choice of law; absent choice, art. 4(1)(a) designates the seller's habitual residence (Spain) for sale of goods. Switzerland: Swiss PIL Act also lets parties choose the law; absent choice, seller's law applies [unverified].
- **CISG (Vienna Convention) [unverified, important]:** Spain, France, Belgium, Luxembourg and Switzerland are all CISG states; for cross-border sales of goods between businesses, the CISG applies automatically unless excluded (CISG art. 6). A clause "droit espagnol" alone is generally read as including the CISG. Decide consciously: either exclude it ("à l'exclusion de la Convention de Vienne du 11 avril 1980") or keep it. Most SME templates exclude it for predictability.
- **French L441-10 for a foreign seller:** L441-10 (payment term caps, mandatory mention of penalty rate and EUR 40 on invoices) is drafted for French commercial relationships; there is legal debate on whether payment-term caps are lois de police (Rome I art. 9) — no authoritative source found. Practical safe route: payment terms ≤ 60 days (complies with both Spanish art. 4 and French caps), state the EUR 40 and the interest rule; this satisfies both regimes. Do not state French-specific rate of "3 × taux d'intérêt légal" or "taux BCE + 10 points" (French default) since Spanish law (ECB + 8) governs; a contractual rate cannot be abusive under art. 9.
- **Jurisdiction [unverified]:** Brussels I bis Regulation (EU) 1215/2012 art. 25 allows a choice-of-court clause (e.g. courts of [ciudad], Spain) for FR/BE/LU; for Switzerland the Lugano Convention 2007 art. 23 does the same.

## 5. Loi Toubon on a B2B website for French customers

### Takeaway
The loi Toubon (art. 2) requires French in the designation, offer and presentation of goods and services and in invoices, and the Culture Ministry says this covers online sales; whether it applies between professionals is debated and not addressed by the Ministry. Since the section is being built in French anyway, the practical answer is: offer, product presentation and CGV in French, and also be able to issue French-language (or bilingual) invoices on request.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 2 requires French "dans la désignation, l'offre, la présentation, le mode d'emploi ou d'utilisation" of goods and services, "ainsi que dans les factures et quittances"; covers goods and services sold in France including imported ones and those on online sales sites; websites are not "lieux ouverts au public" (art. 3 does not apply); translations are allowed alongside French; the page does not address B2B — [Ministère de la Culture, Garantir l'emploi du français](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/thematiques/langue-francaise-et-langues-de-france/agir-pour-les-langues/garantir-l-emploi-du-francais)

### Inferences
- Art. 2 is commonly read as protecting consumers and users; its application to pure B2B offers by a foreign seller is uncertain and enforcement risk is low [unverified]. Belgium, Luxembourg and Switzerland have no equivalent obligation for private B2B websites [unverified].
- Invoices: Spanish invoicing law governs the seller's invoices; a bilingual ES/FR invoice is a low-cost way to satisfy any Toubon-based expectation [inference].

## 6. VAT wording (intra-EU B2B and Switzerland)

### Takeaway
For goods shipped from Spain to a VAT-registered business in France/Belgium/Luxembourg, the supply is an **exempt intra-Community supply** (not a "reverse charge" in the strict sense); the buyer self-accounts for VAT on the intra-Community acquisition in its own country. The invoice should cite the exemption (Spanish: "Entrega intracomunitaria exenta, art. 25 Ley 37/1992" / FR translation), while "Autoliquidation" is the French term for cases where the customer owes the VAT (mainly services). Switzerland: import VAT 8.1% is due on import; who pays depends on the Incoterm (DAP: client; DDP: seller, normally requiring Swiss registration).

### Cited Findings
- French invoice rule: when the acquirer or recipient owes the tax, "la mention "Autoliquidation" doit désormais apparaître sur la facture" (art. 242 nonies A, ann. II CGI, after decree of 24 Apr 2013); intra-Community supplies are referenced to "Article 262 ter I 1°" CGI (exemption) — [Legifiscal (updated 16 July 2026)](https://www.legifiscal.fr/tva/tva-internationale/facture-mentions-obligatoires-operations-internationales.html)
- Legifiscal flags a news item (28 Sept 2026) on new mandatory invoice mentions for international trade in 2027 (content not accessible) — [Legifiscal](https://www.legifiscal.fr/tva/tva-internationale/facture-mentions-obligatoires-operations-internationales.html)
- BOFiP (French tax doctrine) section on mandatory invoice mentions — [BOFiP BOI-TVA-DECLA-30-20-20-30](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/bofip/1531-PGP.html/identifiant=BOI-TVA-DECLA-30-20-20-30-20250108) (not opened in this session)
- Swiss import tax: "The normal tax rate is 8.1%, while certain basic necessities benefit from a reduced rate of 2.6%"; "Import tax is levied on all imported goods, irrespective of the value of the consignment" — [BAZG/FOCBS](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/en/import-taxes-vat-on-imported-goods)

### Inferences
- **Which invoice rules apply [unverified, standard]:** under Directive 2006/112/EC art. 219a, invoicing follows the rules of the Member State where the supply is deemed to take place, i.e. **Spain** for goods dispatched from Spain. Conditions: buyer has a valid VAT number checked in VIES, goods physically leave Spain, seller files the recapitulative statement (modelo 349).

## 7. Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland differences relevant to the same French page

### Takeaway
For Belgium and Luxembourg the same EU framework applies (country of origin for information duties; GDPR; ePrivacy-based cookie consent; intra-EU VAT exemption), so one French page works if it names the local supervisory authorities. Switzerland is outside the EU: no country-of-origin principle, nLPD/nFADP instead of GDPR, a softer cookie regime, import VAT/customs instead of intra-EU exemption, and Lugano instead of Brussels I bis.

### Cited Findings
- Swiss import VAT 8.1% standard / 2.6% reduced — [BAZG/FOCBS](https://www.bazg.admin.ch/en/import-taxes-vat-on-imported-goods)
- CNIL recommendation (France-specific) is the strictest reference point found for banners — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-01/recommandation_cookies_consolidee.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Belgium [unverified]:** information duties in Code de droit économique art. III.74 / XII.6 (implementing e-Commerce Directive), but country of origin means Spanish LSSI governs; cookie consent under Belgian telecom/ePrivacy implementation, supervised by the Autorité de protection des données (APD/GBA), whose guidance also expects a "refuser" option on the first layer; Belgian B2B buyers may also expect a Dutch version (the site already has NL). Late payment: Belgian law of 2 Aug 2002 implements the same directive (EUR 40).
- **Luxembourg [unverified]:** supervisory authority CNPD; cookie rule in the law of 30 May 2005 (art. 4(3)(e)); French is an official language, so the French section serves LU directly.
- **Switzerland [unverified]:** revised FADP (nLPD) in force 1 Sept 2023 applies to processing with effects in Switzerland; duty to inform includes the countries of transfer; Swiss-US DPF in effect since 15 Sept 2024; cookies governed by art. 45c Telecommunications Act (information + opt-out suffices), so the EU banner over-complies; Swiss authority: PFPDT/FDPIC. Mentions légales: no LCEN/LSSI-type mandatory imprint for foreign sites, but Swiss UWG art. 3(1)(s) requires e-commerce operators to give clear identity and contact (incl. address) [unverified] — another reason to publish the address. Jurisdiction clause: Lugano Convention 2007. Swiss French ("Suisse romande") uses CHF; show EUR prices with "hors TVA, hors droits et taxes d'importation".
- **Page-level recommendation:** one French legal page with sections: Mentions légales · Conditions générales de vente (B2B) · Politique de confidentialité · Cookies, with a short country note: « Clients en Belgique, au Luxembourg et en Suisse : les mêmes conditions s'appliquent. Pour la Suisse, voir la rubrique TVA et dédouanement. Autorités de protection des données : APD (Belgique), CNPD (Luxembourg), PFPDT (Suisse). »
